# Revision History

## Purpose

This document records the revision history of the FamilyOS Foundation.

It provides a transparent and traceable record of the evolution of the Foundation document throughout the lifetime of the FamilyOS project.

The revision history supports long-term maintainability, accountability, and historical understanding of the project's guiding principles.

---

# Versioning Policy

The FamilyOS Foundation follows semantic versioning for documentation.

A new version SHALL be published whenever the Foundation is modified.

Version increments SHOULD follow these guidelines:

| Change Type                                        | Version Increment |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| Editorial corrections                              | Patch             |
| Clarifications and non-breaking additions          | Minor             |
| Changes affecting project principles or governance | Major             |

# Change Categories

Changes to the Foundation are classified into the following categories.

## Editorial

Editorial changes improve readability without altering meaning.

Examples include:

* grammar corrections;
* formatting improvements;
* terminology consistency.

Editorial revisions SHOULD result in a patch version increment.

---

## Clarification

Clarification changes improve precision while preserving the original intent.

Examples include:

* additional explanatory text;
* improved examples;
* clearer wording.

Clarification revisions SHOULD result in a minor version increment.

---

## Normative

Normative changes modify project principles, governance, or long-term commitments.

Examples include:

* introducing new guiding principles;
* modifying governance responsibilities;
* changing architectural philosophy;
* revising long-term commitments.

Normative revisions SHOULD result in a major version increment.

---

# Review Process

Every revision SHOULD follow the standard documentation review process.

The review SHOULD verify:

* consistency with the FamilyOS vision;
* alignment with existing ADR, RFC, SPEC, and ENG documents;
* preservation of terminology;
* correctness of references;
* documentation quality.

Significant revisions MAY require architectural review before approval.

---

# Approval

A Foundation revision SHALL be considered approved only after:

* technical review;
* documentation review;
* governance review (when applicable);
* formal acceptance by the project maintainers.

Approval signifies that the revised Foundation becomes the authoritative version.

---

# Deprecation Policy

The FamilyOS Foundation is intended to remain stable.

Individual sections MAY evolve, but complete replacement of the Foundation SHOULD occur only under exceptional circumstances.

Deprecated concepts SHOULD remain documented for historical traceability whenever practical.

---

# Historical Integrity

Previous Foundation versions SHOULD remain accessible through version control.

Historical revisions provide valuable context for understanding:

* architectural evolution;
* governance changes;
* engineering philosophy;
* long-term project direction.

History is considered an important engineering asset.

---

# Relationship to Other Documents

The revision history complements the Foundation by documenting how its principles evolve over time.

Future revisions SHOULD preserve continuity with previous versions while ensuring that FamilyOS continues to adapt responsibly to new requirements and technological evolution.

The Foundation is expected to evolve slowly, deliberately, and transparently.
